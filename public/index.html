    How the converter works

    Choose the city you are converting from, then set a date and time. Every other city in the list shows the same moment in its own local time, with its offset from UTC and how far ahead or behind it is.

    Drag the slider or click any hour in a band to move the time. Each band covers one day in your reference city, so a column lined up across every row is the same instant everywhere. When every city has a working-hours slot in common, the converter names it under the list.

    Your list of cities is saved in this browser. Use Copy link to send someone the exact time and cities you are looking at.

    Common questions

    Does the converter handle daylight saving time?

    Yes. Offsets come from the time zone database built into your browser, which records when each region changes its clocks. If you pick a date after a change, the result uses the offset in force on that date.

    What is the difference between GMT and UTC?

    For everyday use they show the same time. UTC is the modern standard that clocks are set by. GMT is the time zone used in the UK in winter; in summer the UK moves to British Summer Time, one hour ahead.

    Why is IST five and a half hours ahead of UTC?

    India uses a single time zone set to the longitude of central India, which falls halfway between whole hours. Nepal, parts of Australia and a few other places also use half-hour or 45-minute offsets, and the converter shows them exactly.

    Why does an abbreviation like CST give a different city than I expected?

    Some abbreviations stand for more than one zone. CST can mean Central Standard Time in North America or China Standard Time. Searching for a city name is the most reliable way to get the zone you want.

    Is my location sent anywhere?

    No. The converter reads your time zone from your device and does every calculation in your browser. See the privacy policy for how advertising on this site uses cookies.
